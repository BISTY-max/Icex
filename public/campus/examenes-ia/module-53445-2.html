Soluciones y criterios de corrección

Corrección orientativa generada con IA. En las respuestas abiertas puede haber varias soluciones válidas.

Tipo test

  1. D. The best feasible alternative if no deal is reached
    An alternative is useful only if it is feasible and assessed realistically.

  2. C. Responsibilities, timing and implementation checks
    Implementation details make commitments operational.

  3. D. A personal rejection of the negotiator
    Keeping relationship and substance separate prevents misinterpretation.

  4. A. Relevant expertise and capacity
    Matching tasks to capability improves execution.

  5. D. Discuss payment timing as well as price
    Different priorities can make a package better for both sides.

  6. D. Check shared understanding of key points and next steps
    Summaries help verify agreement and unresolved issues.

  7. C. Preparation, choices, evidence and learning across stages
    A cumulative record supports reflection and accountability.

  8. D. Variation of rhythm and intonation
    Vocal delivery influences emphasis and meaning.

  9. A. Trying to understand before evaluating
    Understanding first improves the quality of later evaluation.

  10. A. Check meaning rather than assume a national stereotype
    Cultural frameworks are prompts to investigate, not deterministic labels.

  11. B. Identifying evidence and a method to verify it
    Separate factual uncertainty from preferences and relationships.

  12. D. Interests behind the position
    Interests explain the needs behind a stated demand.

  13. C. Using the behaviour of relevant others as a cue
    It works through perceived conduct or choices of comparable people.

  14. A. Members understand shared interests and differences
    Shared goals do not remove conflicting priorities.

  15. C. Improve perspective-taking
    Understanding an alternative can reduce entrenched positions.

  16. D. Goals, interests, alternatives and constraints
    These elements support disciplined strategy and assessment.

  17. D. Ask tactful clarifying questions
    Meaning should be checked with sensitivity to context.

  18. A. Create space for emphasis and understanding
    Pacing and pauses help listeners process content.

  19. D. Conflict over roles and ways of working
    Conflict can be part of negotiating roles and norms.

  20. D. Explore gently rather than diagnose motives as facts
    Nonverbal cues require interpretation and checking.

  21. B. Avoid accepting a deal worse than its acceptable alternative
    A limit supports disciplined decisions under pressure.

  22. D. Making trade-offs explicit
    Differences in priorities call for negotiation and criteria.

  23. B. Demonstrated competence and reliable delivery
    Performance and systems can underpin confidence.

  24. C. What makes this deadline important for your team?
    A neutral why or what question can uncover underlying constraints.

  25. C. Debrief learning and close the work thoughtfully
    Closure includes reflection on results and team experience.

  26. C. The question, audience and verified key messages
    Effective communication connects relevance, clarity and evidence.

  27. A. Leading the counterpart
    Leading questions constrain or bias responses.

  28. A. Reduce personal defensiveness while examining substance
    Respect can coexist with rigorous disagreement.

  29. C. Objective criteria
    External standards can reduce a battle of wills.

  30. D. State what is known and how the uncertainty will be checked
    Credibility depends on distinguishing verified information from unknowns.

  31. A. Its cost, value to the other side and the overall package
    A concession is part of a negotiation strategy, not an automatic social gesture.

  32. D. Too strong without contextual evidence
    The course reading questions universal applicability across cultural contexts.

  33. C. Interests, influence and relationships relevant to the issue
    Stakeholders differ in stakes and ability to affect outcomes.

  34. A. Responsibilities, norms and ways to handle problems
    Explicit expectations support coordination and accountability.

  35. B. Respecting people while examining the issue rigorously
    Relationship and substantive problem should be handled separately.

  36. B. Time invested in interpersonal connection
    Relationship building may matter before business commitments.

  37. B. Voluntary and active
    People tend to align later actions with commitments they accept.

  38. C. Truthful about the actual limitation
    Ethical influence requires truthful claims.

  39. B. Relevant expertise and credibility
    Relevance and credibility matter more than status alone.

  40. B. Labelling the person's character
    Behaviour-based feedback is more actionable and less personal.

  41. A. A clear choice, reasoning and major risks
    Decision-makers need a supported, bounded course of action.

  42. B. A meaningful benefit or helpful gesture
    Receiving help may create a felt inclination to reciprocate.

  43. A. Decision, relevant evidence and recommendation
    The structure should support the decision the audience needs to make.

  44. B. Replace evidence about the proposal
    A positive relationship does not prove a deal is suitable.

  45. D. Explicit verbal information
    More meaning is expressed directly in the message itself.

  46. C. Evaluate alternatives using a shared basis
    Criteria reduce unproductive conflict over preferred options.

  47. D. Parties value issues differently
    Trades across issues can satisfy different preferences.

  48. C. Clear key messages supported by verified facts
    Communication must be clear, credible and appropriate to the audience.

  49. D. The project failed and identify plausible causes
    Imagining failure can expose risks early.

  50. C. Invite other views and use agreed speaking norms
    Process design can improve participation without personal attack.
