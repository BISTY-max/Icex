Tipo test

  1. C. Generate multiple options
    Divergence opens alternatives before convergence evaluates them.

  2. D. Parties value issues differently
    Trades across issues can satisfy different preferences.

  3. C. Explore gently rather than diagnose motives as facts
    Nonverbal cues require interpretation and checking.

  4. C. Conflict over roles and ways of working
    Conflict can be part of negotiating roles and norms.

  5. D. The thinking process and agenda
    It structures the use of other modes of thinking.

  6. B. A clear choice, reasoning and major risks
    Decision-makers need a supported, bounded course of action.

  7. A. A stated demand rather than the underlying reason
    Asking why can reveal interests below the demand.

  8. B. Too strong without contextual evidence
    The course reading questions universal applicability across cultural contexts.

  9. C. Invite other views and use agreed speaking norms
    Process design can improve participation without personal attack.

  10. D. The question, audience and verified key messages
    Effective communication connects relevance, clarity and evidence.

  11. C. Responsibilities, timing and implementation checks
    Implementation details make commitments operational.

  12. A. Create value through differences in interests and priorities
    Exploring trade-offs can improve joint outcomes.

  13. D. The best feasible alternative if no deal is reached
    An alternative is useful only if it is feasible and assessed realistically.

  14. D. Relevant expertise and capacity
    Matching tasks to capability improves execution.

  15. B. Identifying evidence and a method to verify it
    Separate factual uncertainty from preferences and relationships.

  16. D. What do you mean by reliable delivery in this case?
    Clarification tests meaning instead of assuming it.

  17. C. Interests behind the position
    Interests explain the needs behind a stated demand.

  18. A. Making trade-offs explicit
    Differences in priorities call for negotiation and criteria.

  19. A. Improve perspective-taking
    Understanding an alternative can reduce entrenched positions.

  20. B. Preparation, choices, evidence and learning across stages
    A cumulative record supports reflection and accountability.

  21. D. Check meaning rather than assume a national stereotype
    Cultural frameworks are prompts to investigate, not deterministic labels.

  22. D. Check shared understanding of key points and next steps
    Summaries help verify agreement and unresolved issues.

  23. A. Labelling the person's character
    Behaviour-based feedback is more actionable and less personal.

  24. D. A personal rejection of the negotiator
    Keeping relationship and substance separate prevents misinterpretation.

  25. C. Leading the counterpart
    Leading questions constrain or bias responses.

  26. B. Responsibilities, norms and ways to handle problems
    Explicit expectations support coordination and accountability.

  27. D. Demonstrated competence and reliable delivery
    Performance and systems can underpin confidence.

  28. B. Showing attention and inviting the speaker to continue
    Short signals can support a speaker without taking over.

  29. C. Goals, interests, alternatives and constraints
    These elements support disciplined strategy and assessment.

  30. C. Ask tactful clarifying questions
    Meaning should be checked with sensitivity to context.

  31. D. Discuss payment timing as well as price
    Different priorities can make a package better for both sides.

  32. C. Evaluate and select among options
    Convergence applies criteria to narrow choices.

  33. D. Trying to understand before evaluating
    Understanding first improves the quality of later evaluation.

  34. C. Facts and information needs
    White Hat separates known facts from information still needed.

  35. A. Respecting people while examining the issue rigorously
    Relationship and substantive problem should be handled separately.

  36. B. Objective criteria
    External standards can reduce a battle of wills.

  37. D. Reduce personal defensiveness while examining substance
    Respect can coexist with rigorous disagreement.

  38. B. Explicit verbal information
    More meaning is expressed directly in the message itself.

  39. A. The listener wants a fuller account
    Open questions invite elaboration.

  40. A. Creativity and alternatives
    It encourages novel possibilities.

  41. B. What makes this deadline important for your team?
    A neutral why or what question can uncover underlying constraints.

  42. B. Avoid accepting a deal worse than its acceptable alternative
    A limit supports disciplined decisions under pressure.

  43. A. Debrief learning and close the work thoughtfully
    Closure includes reflection on results and team experience.

  44. D. Feelings and intuitions
