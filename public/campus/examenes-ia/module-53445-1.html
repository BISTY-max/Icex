Soluciones y criterios de corrección

Corrección orientativa generada con IA. En las respuestas abiertas puede haber varias soluciones válidas.

Tipo test

  1. C. Using the behaviour of relevant others as a cue
    It works through perceived conduct or choices of comparable people.

  2. D. Trying to understand before evaluating
    Understanding first improves the quality of later evaluation.

  3. B. Responsibilities, norms and ways to handle problems
    Explicit expectations support coordination and accountability.

  4. A. Explore gently rather than diagnose motives as facts
    Nonverbal cues require interpretation and checking.

  5. B. Clear key messages supported by verified facts
    Communication must be clear, credible and appropriate to the audience.

  6. C. What do you mean by reliable delivery in this case?
    Clarification tests meaning instead of assuming it.

  7. A. Parties value issues differently
    Trades across issues can satisfy different preferences.

  8. B. Generate multiple options
    Divergence opens alternatives before convergence evaluates them.

  9. B. Respecting people while examining the issue rigorously
    Relationship and substantive problem should be handled separately.

  10. A. Evaluate and select among options
    Convergence applies criteria to narrow choices.

  11. D. Acknowledging the feeling without necessarily accepting the claim
    Acknowledgment and agreement are different acts.

  12. A. Debrief learning and close the work thoughtfully
    Closure includes reflection on results and team experience.

  13. D. What makes this deadline important for your team?
    A neutral why or what question can uncover underlying constraints.

  14. D. Voluntary and active
    People tend to align later actions with commitments they accept.

  15. B. Check shared understanding of key points and next steps
    Summaries help verify agreement and unresolved issues.

  16. C. Risks and weaknesses
    Critical judgment examines what might go wrong.

  17. A. Create space for emphasis and understanding
    Pacing and pauses help listeners process content.

  18. B. A stated demand rather than the underlying reason
    Asking why can reveal interests below the demand.

  19. D. Create value through differences in interests and priorities
    Exploring trade-offs can improve joint outcomes.

  20. D. Replace evidence about the proposal
    A positive relationship does not prove a deal is suitable.

  21. B. Time invested in interpersonal connection
    Relationship building may matter before business commitments.

  22. C. The best feasible alternative if no deal is reached
    An alternative is useful only if it is feasible and assessed realistically.

  23. C. Paraphrasing
    The speaker can confirm or correct the interpretation.

  24. C. Avoid accepting a deal worse than its acceptable alternative
    A limit supports disciplined decisions under pressure.

  25. D. Interests behind the position
    Interests explain the needs behind a stated demand.

  26. C. Truthful about the actual limitation
    Ethical influence requires truthful claims.

  27. D. Check meaning rather than assume a national stereotype
    Cultural frameworks are prompts to investigate, not deterministic labels.

  28. D. Discuss payment timing as well as price
    Different priorities can make a package better for both sides.

  29. C. Demonstrated competence and reliable delivery
    Performance and systems can underpin confidence.

  30. B. Variation of rhythm and intonation
    Vocal delivery influences emphasis and meaning.

  31. B. Facts and information needs
    White Hat separates known facts from information still needed.

  32. B. Decision, relevant evidence and recommendation
    The structure should support the decision the audience needs to make.

  33. C. Relevant expertise and capacity
    Matching tasks to capability improves execution.

  34. B. Relevant expertise and credibility
    Relevance and credibility matter more than status alone.

  35. C. Conflict over roles and ways of working
    Conflict can be part of negotiating roles and norms.

  36. A. The project failed and identify plausible causes
    Imagining failure can expose risks early.

  37. B. The thinking process and agenda
    It structures the use of other modes of thinking.

  38. A. Creativity and alternatives
    It encourages novel possibilities.

  39. B. A meaningful benefit or helpful gesture
    Receiving help may create a felt inclination to reciprocate.

  40. A. Feelings and intuitions
    Emotional reactions can be surfaced without treating them as factual proof.

  41. A. State what is known and how the uncertainty will be checked
    Credibility depends on distinguishing verified information from unknowns.

  42. B. Interests, influence and relationships relevant to the issue
    Stakeholders differ in stakes and ability to affect outcomes.

  43. B. Benefits and positive value
    It deliberately looks for advantages and opportunities.

  44. C. The listener wants a fuller account
    Open questions invite elaboration.

  45. C. Ask tactful clarifying questions
    Meaning should be checked with sensitivity to context.

  46. A. Members understand shared interests and differences
    Shared goals do not remove conflicting priorities.

  47. C. Objective criteria
    External standards can reduce a battle of wills.

  48. D. Showing attention and inviting the speaker to continue
    Short signals can support a speaker without taking over.

  49. C. Evaluate alternatives using a shared basis
    Criteria reduce unproductive conflict over preferred options.

  50. C. Explicit verbal information
    More meaning is expressed directly in the message itself.
